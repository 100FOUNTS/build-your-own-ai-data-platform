# Mechanical seal

## Description
The mechanical seal keeps the pumped fluid from escaping along the shaft. It is made of a rotating face and a stationary face, both polished and separated by a microscopic liquid film. It is the component that fails most often from running dry.

## Allowed leakage
A new seal may show an initial drip during run-in. A leak of up to 5 drops per minute is considered acceptable. More than 20 drops per minute, or a continuous leak, requires stopping the pump and replacing the seal.

## Signs before the failure
A gradual drop in discharge pressure, a slight increase in motor current and moisture or crystals appearing around the seal gland usually anticipate the failure by 10 to 16 days.

## Flush plan
Pumps whose fluid contains solids use an API 11 flush plan (recirculation from the discharge to the seal chamber) to cool and clean the faces. Check that the flush line is not blocked before every start.

## Replacement
When installing a new seal, measure the working length against the manufacturer's tolerance (±0.5 mm) and lubricate the elastomers with compatible silicone. Never touch the faces with bare hands.
