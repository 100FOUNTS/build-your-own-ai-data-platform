# Cavitation

## What it is
Cavitation happens when the liquid pressure at the suction falls below its vapor pressure and bubbles form that collapse violently inside the impeller. It erodes the impeller and damages bearings and seals.

## Symptoms
A noise like gravel or marbles hitting the casing, fast oscillations of the discharge pressure, a drop in flow and vibration with a lot of broadband noise. In the plant data it shows up as an increase in the standard deviation of pressure and vibration, even though the mean barely changes.

## Safety condition: NPSH
To avoid cavitation, the available NPSH must exceed the NPSH required by the pump by a minimum margin of 0.5 m. The available NPSH drops with a low suction tank level, high fluid temperature, dirty strainers and partially closed suction valves.

## Corrective actions
1. Fully open the suction valve and clean the strainer.
2. Reduce the flow by partially closing the discharge valve.
3. Raise the suction tank level if possible.
4. If it persists, evaluate an impeller with a lower required NPSH.

Cavitation sustained for more than a week usually ends in impeller failure.
